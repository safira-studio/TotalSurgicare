End-to-end website audit

totalsurgicare.com

Full-site technical audit covering performance, Core Web Vitals, SEO, accessibility (WCAG 2.1 AA), responsive behaviour on mobile and desktop, security headers and functional health — modelled on what Lighthouse and GTmetrix measure, run against the live production site.

Date: 19 Aug 2026 Pages crawled: 54 (100% of reachable site) Deep-tested: 6 key templates Stack: Next.js on Netlify · HTTP/2 · Brotli Browser: Chrome 151, real device, India

Headline: the site is well-built and fast to first byte, but it ships 22.6 MB of unoptimised images, has no structured data at all, fails colour contrast on its main call-to-action, and hides part of its navigation on standard laptop screens.

Nothing is broken at the server level — every one of the 54 pages returns HTTP 200, there are zero console errors, zero broken internal links and CLS is essentially zero. The problems are all in the optimisation and compliance layer, and most of them are a day or two of work each.

5. SEO findings

The biggest SEO gap is structural, not textual: there is no structured data anywhere on the site.

All 54 pages were parsed for application/ld+json. Zero were found. For a multi-city surgical clinic this removes any chance of rich results, doctor/clinic knowledge panels, FAQ snippets and breadcrumb trails, and weakens the E-E-A-T signals Google applies especially hard to Your-Money-Your-Life health content.

SeverityFindingScaleFix
Critical No schema.org structured data on any page54 / 54 pages Add MedicalClinic + LocalBusiness (per city) with address, geo, hours and phone; MedicalProcedure on treatment pages; FAQPage on the Q&A blocks; BreadcrumbList sitewide; Physician for named surgeons.
Serious Canonical tag missing39 / 54 pages The homepage, all /treatment/* pages and all service pages have no <link rel="canonical">. Only /pune, /mumbai and their city-treatment children have one. Add self-referencing canonicals everywhere.

Decide which version should rank, canonicalise the others to it, and differentiate the city pages with genuinely local content (that clinic's surgeons, address, prices, patient stories).
Serious The doctor portal is indexable and duplicates the homepage metadata3 pages /prescription, /prescription/login and /prescription/signup all carry the homepage's exact title and meta description, and each has two <h1> elements. Add <meta name="robots" content="noindex,nofollow"> and unique titles.

WCAG 1.3.1		 Present on every page tested — a shared list component puts buttons where only <li> may go, which breaks list semantics for screen readers. Wrap each button in its own <li>.

7. Mobile & desktop behaviour

Navigation items are unreachable on standard laptop screens.

At a 1298 px viewport the main menu renders items out to x = 1504 px with the container clipped and no horizontal scroll — so "Post Surgery Care", "Elderly Care" and "Orthopaedic" simply cannot be clicked. The desktop nav takes over from the hamburger at 1024 px but does not fit until roughly 1520 px, so every 1280, 1366 and 1440-wide laptop — the most common desktop widths in India — loses part of the menu. /orthopaedic is also missing from the sitemap, so those pages are doubly hard to find.

8. Security, privacy & best practices

SeverityFindingDetail
Critical Marketing trackers fire with no consent gate on a health site Google Tag Manager (GTM-TP73RQVF), GA4, Meta Pixel (2409103159560050) and Google Ads / DoubleClick all load immediately, with no cookie banner anywhere on the site. Because the URL path is sent with every event, Meta receives strings like /treatment/piles-treatment tied to a user — i.e. inferred health data. Under India's DPDP Act 2023 (and GDPR for any EU visitor) this needs prior, informed, granular consent.
Serious No Content-Security-Policy Absent entirely. With four third-party script origins already loaded, a CSP is the main defence against script injection and tag-manager abuse.
Serious No X-Frame-Options / frame-ancestors The site can be framed by any origin — clickjacking risk, and it is a page with an appointment form and a doctor login on it.
Moderate Missing Referrer-Policy and Permissions-Policy Full URLs (including treatment paths) leak to third parties as referrers. Set Referrer-Policy: strict-origin-when-cross-origin and lock down unused browser features.
Moderate HSTS is not complete Strict-Transport-Security: max-age=31536000 is set, but without includeSubDomains or preload.
Minor X-Powered-By: Next.js disclosed Minor information leak; set poweredByHeader: false in next.config.js.
Minor Doctor portal publicly indexable /prescription/login and /prescription/signup are crawlable. Add noindex and consider rate-limiting the signup endpoint.

10. Method & limitations

What was actually run

  • Full crawl — every internal link followed from the sitemap and homepage until exhausted: 54 unique pages, each parsed for metadata, headings, images, links, forms, JSON-LD and response headers.
  • Performance — Navigation Timing, Resource Timing and PerformanceObserver (largest-contentful-paint, layout-shift, longtask) in a real foreground Chrome 151 window at 1298×849.
  • Byte weight — every homepage subresource and all 85 site images re-fetched with cache: "reload" to measure true uncached transfer size and real pixel dimensions.
  • Accessibility — axe-core 4.10.2, tags wcag2a, wcag2aa, wcag21a, wcag21aa, best-practice, on /, /treatment/piles-treatment, /contact, /orthopaedic and /prescription/login.
  • Responsive — rendered and measured at 256 px, 393 px and 1298 px viewports, with computed bounding boxes for overflow and tap-target size.
  • Headers — direct fetches of HTML, CSS, JS, images, robots.txt, sitemap.xml, a deliberate 404 and two manifest paths.

What these numbers can and cannot claim

  • No throttling was applied. Timings were captured on a fast fixed-line connection with a desktop-class CPU. Lighthouse's mobile preset simulates a 4× slower CPU and a throttled 4G link, so real mobile field data will be considerably worse than the lab numbers here.
  • The category scores are modelled, not emitted by Lighthouse. They are computed from the same signals Lighthouse weights (measured CWV, payload, cache policy, axe violations, header and metadata checks) and are intended as a shared frame of reference — treat them as ±10, and re-run PageSpeed Insights after the image work for an official number.
  • Mobile performance is an estimate derived from the measured 2.9 MB first-load payload and the 923 KB LCP image over a typical Indian 4G profile.
  • No form was submitted and no account was created — the appointment and doctor-portal forms were inspected structurally only, so backend validation and email delivery are untested.
  • Field data (CrUX) was not consulted; everything here is lab measurement of the live site on 19 Aug 2026.
